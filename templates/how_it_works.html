Where the data comes from

The model is trained on a public dataset of 614 historical loan applications. That is a small dataset. It is enough to demonstrate a real decisioning pipeline and to give you a directionally useful signal; it is not enough to validate a model for actual credit underwriting, and we do not claim otherwise.

The data also carries whatever bias the original approval decisions contained. A model fitted to past decisions reproduces past patterns, including unfair ones.

What we deliberately don't use

Gender and marital status. Using them as a basis for a credit decision is prohibited under fair-lending rules such as ECOA / Regulation B. We do not collect them and the model never sees them. We measured the cost of leaving them out: on this dataset, none.

Your credit score, as a model input. The training data contains no credit bureau scores, so a model trained on it cannot have learned anything from one. An earlier version of this project manufactured random scores during training while presenting the field as real. Your score is now used only in the affordability and credit rules, where the thresholds are published.

How the explanations are computed

For each input, we re-score your application with that one value replaced by a typical value from the training data. The change in the estimate is that input's contribution. It is exact and reproducible, but it varies one factor at a time — so it cannot capture cases where two inputs only matter together, and the individual contributions will not sum precisely to your total.

The affordability rules

Separate from the model, and just arithmetic. The monthly instalment uses the standard amortisation formula at a reference rate of {{ '%.2f'|format(config.annual_interest_rate * 100) }}% per year. We flag an instalment above {{ (config.max_emi_to_income * 100)|round|int }}% of household income, since that is the ceiling most lenders apply. Credit scores below {{ config.cibil_caution_threshold }} are flagged; above {{ config.cibil_strong_threshold }} is treated as strong.

What this is not

Not a loan offer. Not a credit decision. No lender receives your inputs, and no lender is bound by the result. A real assessment involves verified income, documents and a full bureau report — none of which this tool sees.

For engineers and analysts

The full model card — holdout metrics, confusion matrix, calibration table and fairness audit — is generated by the training run and lives in docs/MODEL.md. The live model's metadata is also served at GET /api/model-card.
